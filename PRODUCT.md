# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Internal Tapestry stakeholders and design peers. Stakeholders need a fast signal that this team is serious about retail experience work. Peers need proof of craft: typography, composition, and motion taste that holds up next to high-end editorial and sport-tech references.

## Product Purpose

A design-team landing surface for Tapestry Design. The job of the first fold is to establish authority and taste before explaining process. Success looks like: a stakeholder feels confident putting this team in front of a brand; a peer studies the hero and wants to understand how it was made.

## Brand Personality

Bold, editorial, high-performance.

Voice is confident and short. Emotion: premium intensity with athletic precision. The interface should feel like a luxury magazine cover that also knows how a product ships.

## Anti-references

- Soft corporate SaaS: cream grounds, purple-to-indigo gradients, rounded marketing cards
- Timid centered stacks with icon-title-body feature grids
- Generic “AI landing” chrome: pill clusters, glassmorphism, glow blobs, hero-metric templates
- Editorial-magazine costume on autopilot (display serif + mono labels + hairline rules) when the brief is sport-tech / typographic sans
- Safe dark-mode-only thinking without a committed color moment

## Design Principles

1. **Apple bar.** The craft test for every surface: would Apple post this on apple.com? If it looks ordinary, normal, AI-generated, or like a WordPress / agency theme demo (floating device mockup, dual meta columns, eyebrow + paragraph kits), it fails. Prefer inevitability over decoration. When in doubt, say the harsh read out loud before polishing.
2. **Type is the product.** Display letterforms carry hierarchy and brand before supporting UI does.
3. **One fold, one idea, then proof.** The hero is a single composition. Explorations also show the section beneath it so the direction has narrative context, not a floating poster.
4. **Color as voice.** Each exploration may commit to a different color strategy (field, collision, kinetic), but never dilute into beige neutrality.
5. **Craft over chrome.** Prefer structure, scale contrast, and photography over decorative cards, badges, and floating promo chips.
6. **People serve the work.** Do not use fashion/lifestyle models as the main key. If a person appears, they exist to hold or frame the product of design (for example a Kate Spade campaign model holding a phone that shows a retail experience mockup). The mockup, interface, or designed object is the subject; the person is supporting context.
7. **Two cores, not two palettes.** Explorations must differ by structural concept (what the hero subject is and how regions are arranged), not by recoloring the same layout.
8. **Peer-proof polish.** Every hero should feel finished enough that a design peer could screenshot it as a reference, not a wireframe.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Aim for WCAG AA contrast on text and interactive targets. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` for drum/roll and cursor motion. Do not rely on color alone for meaning; keep type readable at large scale and meta labels legible at small sizes.
